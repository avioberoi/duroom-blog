Discussion

Can the works a culture has already made tell us where it has not been, when all we measure is how far a candidate sits from them? Yes.

DuROOM search paints pictures through a decoder and keeps the ones that are far from the works before 1900 and faithful (Q7). Far is read by 2 other encoders, the observers (DINOv3 and SigLIP). A picture is faithful when CLIP reads it back close to the vector we asked for. From 5 works painted before 1900 the search painted 4,380 far and faithful pictures. Most are on the manifold, nearly all of them where art went only after 1900. From different starts the search arrives at the same later works. The other 331 are off the manifold (Q11). How much of this is discovery, and how much is the decoder recalling later art?

The measure also notices the real works after 1900. Take a work made after 1900, find its 15 nearest works from before 1900 in CLIP, and average the distances to them. This is its score. Score each work before 1900 the same way, against the others. The bar is the score that 99% of them lie below. A later work scores higher than an earlier one about 9 times in 10.1 About 1 in 20 works from 1900 to 1959 is over the bar, and 1 in 4 from 1960 on (Q6). So, as CLIP reads it, the century did move away from its past. Most later works still stay under the bar.

What did the search find?

From 5 works painted before 1900, the search painted 4,380 pictures that are far and faithful, and 4,049 of them are on the manifold. A picture is on the manifold when a real work sits within the manifold line of it in at least one observer, and off the manifold when none does in either (Q7). 3,850 of the 4,049 have a work dated 1900 or later within the line and none dated before 1900. So art went there only after 1900.2 1,314 of the 3,850 also have a large leftover share, i.e., the part of a picture that the 32 main directions of the works before 1900 miss (Q11).

331 are off the manifold, with no real work of any date within the manifold line in either observer. Their nearest real work sits at a median distance of 0.88 in DINOv3, where the line is 0.76. Of the 331, 227 come from one start (Q11).

Told the real score, the loop has about twice as many hits as with random scores, and more at every start. Its 2 controls are the same loop with something taken out (Q12). The random-score loop is told random scores. The no-step loop is told random scores too and tries no new vector. From 5 starts the search kept 120 hits, the random-score loop 65 and the no-step loop 40.3 Of the 9,600 pictures each painted, 4,380, 1,534 and 2,708 are far and faithful. 1,314, 69 and 13 are on the manifold with a later work within the line, none dated before 1900, and a large leftover share. Off the manifold the three have 331, 187 and 107, the search more in total, though not at every start.

Left free, the search goes far, and none of its far pictures is faithful. The free search is DuROOM search with faithful only recorded, not required, no restart and a larger first step. None of its about 2,200 admitted pictures is faithful (Q12).

Of the searches we ran, those that restart paint faithful pictures, and the one that does not paints almost none. Nearly every picture of DuROOM search and its 2 controls is faithful, 9,352 to 9,597 of 9,600, where the free search painted 79 faithful pictures in 5,760. No run switches the restart off alone, and the free search also has a larger step and does not require faithful (Q12). With no search at all, the pictures painted from simple requests not aimed at a later work are far and faithful 6 times in 1,270 (Q9). Each loop paints over 1,000 such pictures.

With no decoder, following the score in CLIP from an earlier work does not lead toward later works. Five gradient methods and CMA-ES each walked from an earlier work’s vector until its score was over a CLIP bar of the same kind. At that point its nearest real works were later ones about as often as the nearest real works of an ordinary earlier work are.4 This test is reported here only.

We also notice a pattern among real works. Take a work’s 100 nearest earlier works in CLIP, their mean, and the 39 directions those works share most. A later work’s offset from that mean lies less in those 39 directions than an earlier work’s does at the same distance.5

Do the starts meet?

On the manifold they do: from 5 different works the search arrives at the same later works. For each of the 3,850 pictures, take its nearest real work dated 1900 or later within the manifold line, in each observer. The pictures reach 492 such works. 104 of them are reached from 3 or more of the 5 starts, and 32 from all 5. Those 104 hold 3,089 of the 3,850 pictures. Gene Davis’s Flamingo (1965) alone is reached by 1,245 of them, from every start (Q11). The controls meet less, or not at all: 516 of the random-score loop’s 1,247 pictures sit at works reached from 3 or more starts, and 0 of the no-step loop’s 1,714.6

Off the manifold they do not. An off-the-manifold picture’s nearest off-the-manifold picture from another start sits at a median distance of 1.11 in DINOv3, against 0.52 for one from its own start.7 In the runs scored against today’s reference, 2,048 repainted works of every date (see the next section), we also tested whether pictures from 3 or more of the 5 starts lie close together in one place off the manifold. They do not, for any loop. Among real works from 1970 on the test finds no such place either, so it cannot say whether one exists (Q11).

Discovery or recollection?

A later work within the manifold line says where a picture sits, and most far and faithful pictures of any procedure have one. 88 in 100 of the search’s far and faithful pictures have later works within the manifold line and no earlier ones. So do 81 in 100 of the random-score loop’s, 63 in 100 of the no-step loop’s, 82 of the 94 far and faithful repaints of real works from 1970 on, and 31 of 32 pictures painted from requests that come from noise images. With no search, works from before 1900, repainted and read back 6 times, end with the median year of their nearest real works past 1900 (Q12). The decoder and the encoders, trained on later images too, pull pictures toward later works.8 So a picture with a later work within the manifold line can be a recollection.

A discovery would lie off the manifold of all the art we have, and the search paints there. Off the manifold, no work of our corpus lies within the manifold line of a picture. So we ran the loops scored against today’s reference, where far means far from all the art we have (Q11). Of the search’s 9,600 pictures there, faithful or not, 1,716 are off the manifold, against 57 and 135 for its controls. 1,047, 7 and 63 are eligible: faithful, off the manifold, and read as away from the repainted works by AIMv2 too, the encoder kept out of the loop.9 By our measure these pictures sit where none of our works has been, which is what the question asks for.

I see later art’s looks on the manifold and no new kind of picture off it, against the corpus we have. The pictures we show on the manifold are all-over patterns, mostly stripes, weaves and swirls, each beside a later abstract work of a similar look. Those we show off the manifold are grey masks, crowds, a lamp, a helmet with goggles and a black and yellow figure (Q11).

Both the count and the reading are bounded by what we have. Off the manifold means off the 172,171 works of our corpus, and a picture can only be what this decoder can paint.

Where does this sit beside earlier work?

The check against a known future is the one used in studies of the new: a song or a paper is scored against a past, and the score is checked against what came later (Askin and Mauskapf 2017; Shi and Evans 2023; Uzzi et al. 2013) (Related work). On art the check holds: the measure notices the later works, and most of what the search paints far from the past sits among the art that came later.

Our ranking of later works above earlier ones agrees in kind with networks that order WikiArt’s paintings in time with no dates given (Elgammal et al. 2018), and with a painting’s date read well from its CLIP vector (Kim et al. 2026). Two cautions raised about them apply to us too. A smooth trend in a curated corpus can come from the curators’ selection (Lee et al. 2020), and our bar favours new media and does not separate a new medium from a new date (Q6). A corpus read through a model is also read through its training data (Impett and Offert 2022), and every loop’s pull toward later art is that training showing.10

Novelty alone can go anywhere, as the noisy TV holds an agent rewarded for what it cannot predict (Burda et al. 2019). Our free search is much like that case. The decoder did not hold it back, though edits through a trained generator have stayed among the images it can make (Zhu et al. 2016). In DuROOM search, faithful plays the part of a minimal criterion (Lehman and Stanley 2010), with one difference: a picture that is not faithful is not scored zero; it only cannot be kept (Related work). With the restart nearly every picture is faithful, the controls’ too, so faithful cannot credit the search over its controls (Q12).

A search for artificial-life simulations scored in a foundation model’s space found known forms such as flocking again (Kumar et al. 2025). Ours mostly lands among art that exists too, the art dated 1900 or later. Our restart also replaces the move by which CMA-ES takes its centre toward the better points it tried (Hansen 2016). So the restart puts our loop closer to an older picture of how new things arise than to CMA-ES: variations made without knowing which will succeed, of which some are kept (Campbell 1960).

Strengths and limits

The corpus is frozen at 1900 and the bar is built only from works before it, so the later works are a future the bar never saw. The search’s bar is of the same kind, read by the observers on repainted pictures, and only 3 in 1,000 repainted works before 1900, other than those the bar is set on, are far (Q7). The search is set against 2 controls with the same decoder and budget, from the same 5 starts (Q12). A search no better than a control would have more hits than that control at all 5 starts at most 1 time in 32.

It is one corpus, and which recent art WikiArt holds is its own choice. There is one decoder, and it and the 3 encoders were trained on later images,11 so the freeze holds for the corpus only. Every five-start count is one run per start, and runs move: the same command from one start, on two types of graphics card, admitted 30 and 66 pictures (Q12). So the sizes of these counts are not settled. I rated a sample of hits blind, by eye, and my rating does not tell the search from the random-score loop. By eye, a second viewer and I chose the pictures shown.

The works made after 1900 are one future among those the past allowed, so they tell us that far and faithful is possible, and they cannot tell us where else it is. A search could be right about a future that did not happen, and we could not tell.

Future directions

Next we would train a decoder only on works before 1900 and repaint the works the bar is set on, and the real later works, with it (Q12). A blind rating of the pictures by other viewers is planned, subject to IRB review.

If a decoder that has only seen the past also drifts toward the later works when it repaints its own pictures, some of where art went was already in where it had been. If it does not, what we watched was memory. Neither answer would be clean, as CLIP and the 2 observers keep their training on later images. And maybe that is the question we should have started with, i.e., when a system frozen in the past finds something far from it, how would we tell a discovery from a recollection, without a future to check it against?
