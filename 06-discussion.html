Discussion

Can the works a culture has already made tell us where it has not been, when all we measure is how far a candidate sits from them? Our answer is in the Introduction. Here we ask what to make of what we found (Results overview).

Do later works sit far from the works before 1900?

More often than earlier works do. The Data chapter shows it before any picture is painted. In CLIP, a basis from the works before 1900 keeps about 9 points less of the later works than a random basis does (Q2). In CLIP, from 1900 to the 1960s each decade leaned further in one direction, and then held there. The decades moved first where Cubism and abstraction sit, then where Minimalism and installation sit (Q3). In CLIP, most works from 1960 on sit where earlier works are almost absent, and a score built only from the works before 1900 ranks a later work above an earlier one about 90% of the time in DINOv3 and SigLIP (Q6). No decoder and no search is involved in any of this.

But far alone does not lead to later works. Most later works stay under the bar, noise is far too, and with no decoder, following the score in CLIP from an earlier work does not lead toward later works (Q12). So far tells us where the works before 1900 are sparse. It does not tell us what sits there (Q7).

How does our search find far and faithful pictures?

It paints and measures, and keeps to pictures (i.e., restarts from what the decoder did paint). The works give a search no reliable rule for where a far picture will come from (Q1) and no formula for where art went next (Q9). But a straight walk from the older works toward the 1960s never runs empty of works in CLIP (Q5), and the decoder follows a moved vector part of the way (Q8). So we can paint a place and measure where the picture lands. The free search, left to follow distance alone, goes far and brings back nothing far and faithful. DuROOM search restarts each round from its own pictures and keeps only faithful ones (Q12). The search moves in CLIP, as the decoder paints from a CLIP vector. Far is read on the painted picture, as moving a vector changes its CLIP distance whether or not the picture changes. It is read by 2 encoders the search does not move in (Q7).

Step by step: we build the search up, then take parts away (Q12).

  1. No search. 6 kinds of simple request: 0.5% of their pictures are far and faithful (requests read from noise images reach 20%, Q9).
  2. A single walk with the score. One picture at a time, kept only when it is faithful and scores higher: 25%. So a single walk already paints such pictures, where the simple requests almost never do.
  3. Evolutionary search with the score. DuROOM search paints a population of 32 pictures a round, and the next round starts from the 4 farthest faithful ones: 46%. The whole loop paints 1.8 times as many as the single walk.1
  4. The same search with selection at random. The same loop told random scores: 16%. The told score is the one thing changed (the step sizes are the search’s own, replayed): with it, the search paints 2.9 times as many.
  5. Selection at random and no variation. Each round only repaints its centre and restarts: 28%. The one change is the step: with no score to pick by, the loop that steps paints fewer, in sum and at 3 of the 5 starts. A loop that only restarts from its own pictures already paints more than half as many as the search.

The score chooses where the loop starts again. The works give a search no single map of directions to steer by (Q1), and a place has to be painted before we know how far it is (Q5). So the score is read on pictures already painted, and the next round starts from the 4 farthest faithful ones. In the loop with no step that is the only job the told score has, and told the real score that loop admits about 2.5 times as many pictures.2 No loop paints a far and faithful picture in its first 4 rounds (appendix). At least 97% of each loop’s pictures are faithful.3

In every loop, most far and faithful pictures land among later works. Of the search’s, 88% sit where art went only after 1900 (a work dated 1900 or later is within the manifold line of them, and none dated before). So do 81% and 63% of its controls’.4 Of those that the art before 1900 also describes badly, the search paints 19 times as many as with random scores. The Data chapter fits it: in CLIP the way from the older works toward the 1960s never runs empty of works (Q5), and most works from 1960 on sit where earlier works are almost absent (Q6).5

Every loop’s pictures lean the way the later works lean, and so do pictures painted from noise. The shift of Q3 is the way the later works lean on average. Pictures painted from noise images, from random requests anywhere on the sphere, by the free search and by the 3 loops lean as much as repainted works of 1900 to 1969. Requests built from the old works do not.6 A lean says which way a picture sits from the mean of the works before 1900, not how far. It does not say the picture went where art went.

Why do different starts arrive at the same works?

Every run ends in the same region of CLIP. Q6 split CLIP’s space into 32 regions. All 15 runs, the 3 loops from each of the 5 starts, end in one of them, a region of 3,950 works of which 1.6% are from before 1900. By round 12 the centre is in a region where under 10% of the works are from before 1900, in all but one run, and it does not go back.7 So all 3 loops go to the same part of the space, with the score or without it. That alone does not bring the starts to the same works: the random-score loop is there as long as the search, and its starts share works less often.

Where the old works are scarce, the score changes how often a picture is far and faithful. All but 3 of the 3 loops’ 8,622 far and faithful pictures lie in regions where under 10% of the works are from before 1900. There, 49% of the search’s pictures are far and faithful, against 18% of the random-score loop’s and 34% of the no-step loop’s. In all 3 loops the observers’ score of a round’s feasible pictures climbs until round 10 and then levels off. Only the search’s 4 picks score above the rest of their round.8 A typical move of the centre has 4 to 5 times as much of its squared length in the 39 directions that the 100 nearest works from before 1900 share as a random direction would, in all 3 loops.9

On the manifold, pictures from different starts often arrive at the same later works, and each start also keeps works of its own. Off it we found no place where they meet. From 5 starts the search took most of its far and faithful pictures to the same later works, stripe painting and op art. Of its pictures that sit where art went only after 1900, 80% are at works reached from 3 or more of the 5 starts. At equal size that is 62%, against 37% of the random-score loop’s (Q11, Q12). Off the manifold, pictures from different starts lie apart. The search’s pictures off the manifold are painted in a few stretches of rounds, earlier than its other far and faithful pictures. At the Fra Angelico start, which has 69% of them, all 227 were painted in 22 rounds in a row.10 The same rule (do pictures from 3 or more starts lie close together?), with random groups of artists in place of the starts, finds no such place among real works from 1970 on that are far from the art before 1970, though it finds places among those that are not far (appendix). Far real works lie apart from each other too, so pictures that lie apart off the manifold do not by themselves show that the search missed a shared place.

What part does the decoder play?

A picture counts as far and faithful only when it reads back within the faithful line of its request (Q7). The search and its controls all use the same decoder, so they differ in the loop and not in the decoder. And on one repaint of a real work’s own vector, old stays old: of 1,000 works from before 1900, each repainted from its own vector, 99% are near the works before 1900 and faithful (appendix). A second decoder, unCLIP, repaints a work about equally near it (on 256 works, Q8). The far and faithful later works, repainted, mostly sit where art went only after 1900 too, as do all but one of the far and faithful pictures painted from requests read from noise images.11

Scored against all the art we have, the search paints faithful pictures off the manifold over 10 times as often as its controls in sum (Q11). Off the manifold says only that no work of our corpus sits within the manifold line of a picture in either observer.12

To our eye, the search’s pictures read as a picture of something about as often as the others do. Off the manifold they look less like the art we have.13

Where does this sit beside earlier work?

Our check against a known future (freeze the corpus at 1900, then look at what came after) is the one used in studies of the new: a song or a paper is scored against a past, and the score is checked against what came later (Askin and Mauskapf 2017; Shi and Evans 2023; Uzzi et al. 2013). A smooth trend in a curated corpus can come from the curators’ selection (Lee et al. 2020), and ours is curated too. Novelty alone can go anywhere, as an agent rewarded for what it cannot predict gets stuck at a noisy TV (Burda et al. 2019), and our free search is much like that case. A search for artificial-life simulations scored in a foundation model’s space found known forms such as flocking again (Kumar et al. 2025), and ours mostly lands among art that exists too, the art dated 1900 or later (Q11).

Strengths and limits

The corpus is frozen at 1900 and the bar is built only from works before it, so the later works are a future the bar never saw, though they helped choose the score (Q6). The search is set against 2 controls with the same decoder and budget, from the same 5 starts (Q12). Far is scored in DINOv3 and SigLIP. With far read in CLIP instead, the search has more pictures far in CLIP and faithful than the random-score loop at every start and than the no-step loop at 4 of the 5, as with the observers (appendix). With far read in AIMv2, the one encoder outside the loop, it has 1.13 and 1.16 times as many as its controls, and more at 4 of the 5 starts against each (appendix). CLIP is inside the loop (the requests are CLIP vectors, the centre restarts from CLIP readings, and faithful is read in it). So neither reading confirms the lead from outside (Q12).

We have one corpus, and every loop ran on one decoder. Every rate here rests on one run per start, and runs move (Q12). We chose the pictures shown by eye, or drew them at random.

The works made after 1900 are one future among those the past allowed. A search could be right about a future that did not happen, and we could not tell.

Future directions

Next we would train a decoder only on works before 1900 and repaint the works the bar is set on, and the real later works, with it (Q12). A blind rating of the pictures by other viewers is planned, subject to IRB review.
