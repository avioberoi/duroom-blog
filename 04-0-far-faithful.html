Q7 What do far, faithful, on and off the manifold mean?

Say we want a picture unlike the works painted before 1900. It has to be far, as by our measure a picture that is not far is not new. And it has to be faithful, as the search moves vectors but is scored on pictures, so a picture that does not come back near what we asked for tells us nothing about the place we asked about. A picture can be far just because the decoder ignored its vector, which it does readily (Q8), so we need both at once (Figure 1).

Far. The reference is a sample of the works before 1900, as the decoder repaints them. For each picture we find its 15 nearest reference pictures and take the mean of those distances, its score. This is the absolute kind of far of Q5, as only that kind can say that a picture has few others around it. Each reference picture is scored the same way, against the others, with its own artist left out. The bar is the score that 99% of them lie below. A picture is far when its score is over the bar in both observers (DINOv3 and SigLIP, encoders the search does not move in). Far says only that known works are sparse around a picture, and not that it is art.1 A place is unexplored when it is far in this sense.

So are later works far? 5.5% of the repainted works from 1900 to 1969 are, and 17% of those from 1970 on, against 0.3% of the repainted works before 1900 (Figure 1; Q6 asks this of the works’ own vectors). So a later work is more often far, though most later works are still not.23

Faithful. A picture is faithful when it comes back near its request, i.e., the vector we hand the decoder (Q8). Its fidelity is the chord between the request and the CLIP reading of the picture, so the lower it is, the more faithful the picture. Each reference picture has a fidelity too, against its own vector. The faithful line is the fidelity that 99% of the reference pictures lie below. A picture whose fidelity is over the faithful line is not faithful, i.e., it did not come back near what we asked for. Faithful says nothing of whether a picture is good, coherent, close to real art or new. As with the bar, one faithful line serves every picture, and it is harder on later works: 7.4% of the repainted works from 1970 on are not faithful, against 1.0% of those from before 1900.4

Why one bar, and why 2 observers?

Why one bar for every region? One bar serves the whole reference, though the works are spaced differently from region to region (Q5). So far means in a sparse part of the map, and not unusual for its own region. Split the reference into 4 regions, and the sparsest one holds all but one of the 21 reference pictures over the DINOv3 bar (Table 1). We tried 2 local bars on repainted real works. A bar scaled to each picture’s own neighbourhood finds 0.8% of the repainted works of 1900 to 1969 far and faithful, and 0.1% of the earlier works. A bar per region finds 1.6 to 2.5% of the later works, and 0.1 to 0.2% of the earlier ones. The one bar finds 5.1% of the later works and 0.2% of the earlier ones. So a local bar catches a sixth to a half as many later works as the one bar, and we keep the one bar.56

Why 2 observers? With 2, more of the later works are far, and no more of the earlier ones. On the works’ own vectors, requiring far in both observers calls far 0.29% of the held-out works before 1900 and 16% of the later works. One observer alone, cut so that it also calls far 0.29% of the earlier works, calls far 12% of the later works, in DINOv3 or in SigLIP. CLIP alone calls far 7.1% of the later works. So 2 observers find about a third more.7

On and off the manifold

Far is measured against the past, and on and off against the art we have today. The search does not use this label; its pictures are read with it afterwards. The manifold is the art we have (Introduction). For each picture we find its nearest real work, of any date, and take that distance. We measure 2,048 repainted real works of every date, today’s reference, the same way, each to its nearest other real work. The manifold line is a pair of distances, one per observer, set together so that 99% of the 2,048 have their nearest real work within it in at least one observer.8 Like the bar, it is one line for the whole corpus. A picture is on the manifold when its nearest real work is within the manifold line in at least one observer, and off the manifold when it is within the line in neither (Figure 2). We say it of any picture, a real work repainted as well as a picture a search painted.

Beside the label we report a picture’s leftover share, as Q11 uses it to ask whether the art before 1900 describes a picture badly.9 In each observer, it is the share of the picture’s squared distance from the average of the repainted works before 1900 that their 32 main directions miss. A leftover share is large when it is over the leftover share that 99% of the reference pictures lie below, in both observers. Q11 counts the far and faithful pictures on the manifold by the dates of the real works within the manifold line, and how many of them have a large leftover share.

Why the nearest real work? We tested 3 readings of “a real work is near” on pictures whose answer we know, and the nearest real work within the manifold line is the only one that keeps nearly every real work on and can still call a picture off.1011

So a picture is far and faithful against the works before 1900, and on or off the manifold against the art we have today. Next, Q8 asks whether the decoder paints what we ask.
